Metronome and BPMEar TrainingViolin

Practice tools — metronome, tuner, ear-training, play-along — raise practice efficiency sharply. Chosen badly, they waste it just as sharply: more features, more pop-ups, less focus.

Three filters

1. Works offline: nothing breaks a session like notifications, loading screens or logins. Offline is the floor. 2. Gives feedback: good tools tell you right/wrong, fast/slow, high/low — not just encouragement points. Clearer feedback, faster progress. 3. Stays out of the way: open it and the function is there — no three-screen ads, no membership prompts. Practice windows are already short.

How to use the four kinds

ToolRight useCommon misuse
Metronomebuild steadiness slowly (see "Why Practise Slowly")opening it only when things fall apart
Tunercalibrating, checking intonationstaring at the numbers all session
Ear trainingfive minutes daily, steady as waterone weekend hour of cramming
Play-along / slow-downcopy at reduced speed, then step back upliving at half speed forever

Two things tools cannot replace

  • A teacher's diagnosis: a tool tells you "this note is flat"; only a person tells you "because your wrist is tense";
  • Your own attention: when the tool says "correct", still ask — can I hear why it is correct? Tools are crutches; the goal is walking.

A footnote from history

Two centuries ago, the "practice app" was a printed book of easy pieces compiled for beginners — an evergreen genre in publishing ever since. The containers change; content designed for practice never does.

Listen: an everyday metronome setting

Around eighty is a comfortable starting point for much practice material. Let every beat sit still first.
