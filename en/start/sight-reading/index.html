How to Practise Sight-Reading

Three habits — eyes ahead, rhythm first, a little new material daily

Related

Sight-ReadingNote ValuesPiano

Sight-reading — performing a score the first time you see it — is often treated as a talent. It is a set of teachable skills that most players never take apart.

Take the definition apart first

  • Eyes off the hands (mostly): the hands' sense of place runs independent of sight;
  • No stopping: mistakes included;
  • Near-performance speed: not endless hunting for notes, but moving on.

Of the three, the second is hardest — it runs against everything normal practice teaches.

Three habits, in order

1. Eyes ahead of hands. While playing this bar, your eyes are already scanning the *shape* of the next (leaps or steps, accidentals). Drill: slow click, and make yourself look at the coming bar before each downbeat. 2. Rhythm before notes. The discipline of sight-reading: drop or miss a note if you must, never break the flow. A break cuts the information chain; a wrong note is just noise in the data. 3. A little new material daily. Sight-reading runs on the total number of *first meetings*. Five minutes of yesterday-untouched material daily beats one hour a week — the key is "new"; revisiting adds nothing.

Choosing material: two grades down

Pick material two grades below your comfortable level. One standard only: a first read that flows 90% clean. Material that is too hard forces you to memorise it — that is no longer sight-reading.

Chorale scores, hymns and simple instrumental miniatures are ideal — clear voices, moderate density.

Two common misreadings

  • "Sight-reading means reading fast": it means reading without stopping. Speed is a by-product;
  • "A good sight-reader needs no memorising": different skills — sight-reading opens the road with the eyes, memorising with memory. Train them separately.

Listen: the plainest shape of sight-reading material

Beginner sight-reading melodies mostly look like this — steps with gentle rises and falls, an occasional small leap. Hear its shape once, then picture the eyes travelling one note ahead.
