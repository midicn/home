Choosing Online Music Resources

Three sieves — verifiable source, complete structure, built to last

1 min read26 characters条目Updated 2026-09-28

resourcesonlinechoosing

Related

Ear TrainingHow to PractisePiano

Online music resources reach choice paralysis: video tutorials, score libraries, Q&A communities, open courses, datasets. The problem was never scarcity — it is judging, in thirty seconds, whether a resource deserves your investment.

Three sieves

1. Verifiable source: does it dare to say what it is based on? Which edition of the score? A second source for the claim? The vaguer it sounds, the more you should worry. 2. Complete structure: is it pearls, or a necklace? Scattered tips rescue emergencies; systematic learning needs a system — read the table of contents first. 3. Built to last: downloadable, printable, citable, offline-readable — worth far more than anything locked inside a platform. A blunt but accurate test — will it still exist in five years?

A self-check for this site

You can bring the same three sieves to this website and the knowledge matrix behind it:

  • Verifiable source: every entry lists its sources; every track example shows provenance and licence tier via "details";
  • Complete structure: starter guides, theory, instruments, history, composers — separate sites woven into one net;
  • Built to last: text is CC BY 4.0 — copy it, adapt it, republish with attribution.

We hold ourselves to the same standards — which is how we hope you pick everything.

Listen: a sentence to recognise by ear

Music has its structures too — the "ii–V–I" ending, the most common full stop in harmony. Recognising it means you are already listening for structure — the same skill you use to sift knowledge.
