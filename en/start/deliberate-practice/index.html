Practice That Works — Three Moves

Name the problem, narrow the range, get instant feedback — practice that compounds

Related

Slow PracticeHow to PractisePiano

"I have practised for three years" — the most deceptive word in that sentence is "practised". Same three years: one person solved a thousand problems; another played the same pass a thousand times.

Three moves turn practice into progress.

Move one: name the problem (small enough to solve)

Practise in units of problems, not pieces:

  • ❌ "Today I will practise the sonata" — too big to start;
  • ✅ "The shift in bar 8 buzzes" — specific, testable, solvable tonight.

A usable self-check: if you told this problem to someone else, would they get it at once? If yes, it is specific enough.

Move two: narrow the range

Once found, squeeze the practice down to where the error actually happens — often not the whole bar, but the move between two notes, one shift, one fingering.

Narrow until ten tries ten successes, then widen by one circle (add the bar before and after). Narrow range is what makes repetition count.

Move three: instant feedback

Practice is "deliberate" when every step can be judged immediately:

FeedbackWhat it checks
Earstuning, tone, buzzing
Metronomeevenness, accidental speeding
Recordingon playback you become your own teacher — many problems become obvious
Mirror or videomotion and relaxation (players overestimate their own relaxation)

A loop you can copy: three passes

1. Play through once and mark one single sorest spot; 2. Take it apart and fix it slowly (see "Why Practise Slowly"); 3. Put it back and run through again — then take the next problem.

One at a time: catching three is catching none.

Three common misreadings

  • "Long practice is good practice": time is only a container — what matters is what gets put in it;
  • "Enough repetitions and it will come": repetition engraves what you actually play — errors included;
  • "Keep going through the mistake": every run-through with the error is a vote for the error.

Listen: one figure at two speeds

The same notes at 60 and at 132 — deliberate practice starts at 60, where full attention is still possible.
