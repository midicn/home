Start Here — a Beginner's Guide to Music

Thirty guides, one question each — and every one tells you where to go next

"You want to learn music and have no idea where to start" — this guide is written for exactly that.

Thirty short pieces, one question each; every one ends with a "Next" that sends you where you need to go. Start from whichever matches your situation — no need to read in order.

Getting started from zero (6)

  • First Steps — Where to Begin with Music — pick a direction, pick a starting line
  • Can Adults Still Learn Music? — adult advantages and honest obstacles
  • How Long Should You Practise Each Day? — frequency beats duration
  • Theory First or Instrument First? — not a queue but a loop
  • "Do I Have Talent?" — The Wrong Question — why the question cannot be answered
  • Can You Learn Music Without Reading Notation? — "yes" first, then "should you learn it"

By goal (8)

  • Want to Learn Music Theory? Start Here — four floors, start from what is audible
  • Want to Learn an Instrument? Your First Move — work backwards, then test
  • Want to Understand Classical Music? — three flights: atmosphere, clues, structure
  • Want to Get into Jazz? — begin with one gesture, the swing
  • Want to Write and Arrange Music? Start Here — finish one small piece first
  • Want to Explore Music History? — collect each era's earprint first
  • Want to Study Music Seriously? — start from one answerable question
  • Music with Kids at Home — Where to Start — connection, not early learning

Choosing an instrument (5)

  • What Instrument Does My Budget Buy? — three lanes, three strategies
  • Acoustic Piano, Digital Piano or Keyboard? — three needs, three designs
  • Starting Guitar — Which One First? — steel, nylon or electric
  • Are Uncommon Instruments Worth It? — scarcity is an asset, past three checks
  • How to Shop for a Used Instrument — bring a checklist, earn the discount

Practice method (4)

  • Practice That Works — Three Moves — name it, narrow it, feedback it
  • Why Practise Slowly? — practice with the brain present
  • How to Practise Sight-Reading — eyes ahead, rhythm first, new material daily
  • Memorising Music — Build More Than One Index — from chain to net

Tools and software (4)

  • Choosing Music Software — three kinds, one workhorse each
  • Choosing a Practice App — offline, feedback, unobtrusive
  • Free vs Paid — What You Are Really Buying — read the cost structure first
  • Choosing Online Music Resources — three sieves

Common myths (3)

  • The Most Common Myths About Learning Music — ten claims, ten replies
  • What Is "Musical Talent", Really? — unpack it into five things
  • Must You Take Graded Exams? — a tool, not the goal

How to use this guide

1. Choose by situation, not by order — find where you are stuck and start there; 2. Do after reading — each "Next" is an action, not just a link; 3. Come back any time — when stuck, switching to another piece beats pushing through.
