Split the question in two: Can you learn without notation? Yes. Should you pick it up? Yes, recommended.

First: which "notation" do you mean?

Notation is not one thing but a family of tools: staff notation (classical and academic), numbered notation (common in Chinese pop and folk music), guitar tablature, and various instrument-specific systems.

Many people who say "I can't read music" mean "I can't read staff notation" — while they already read numbered notation, or can learn a song from a slow video. Not reading staff and not knowing music are two different things.

How far you can go without reading: the honest version

  • Roads that stay open: singing (oral traditions are full of singers who do not read), playing popular tunes (copying phrase by phrase from a slowed-down demo), improvising inside chord shapes.
  • Walls you will meet: every new piece depends on someone "translating" it for you; structure stays invisible; playing with others costs extra effort to coordinate.

In one line: you can get far without reading — but someone else has to pave the road.

What reading actually buys you

It is not mysterious. Two ideas cover the core: height on the lines = pitch; shape of the note = duration. Once that lands, the "code" becomes a map.

  • Cheaper than you think: a couple of hours to recognise the basic symbols, then a little practice each week while you use it.
  • A direct payoff: one score = the ability to teach yourself any piece that has one. No more waiting for tutorials.
  • A hidden payoff: notation lays music out flat on the page — structure becomes visible at a glance. Some things stay blurry after three listens and become obvious in ten seconds of looking.

A route for non-readers

1. Do not let it block your start: begin with listening and imitation; joy comes first. 2. From week three, add 15 minutes of reading a week: pitches first (lines, spaces, ledger lines), then rhythm (the durations of the basic note shapes). 3. Use songs you can already sing as your textbook: singing along is built-in error correction — you hear instantly whether what you read matches the melody in your head.
