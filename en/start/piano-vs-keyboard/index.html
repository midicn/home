"Piano or keyboard?" The answer depends first on which thing you actually want. The names sit close together; the design goals do not.

Three things, three designs

Answer three questions first

1. Are you learning "piano" or "keyboard"? Classical repertoire and touch need weighted keys (acoustic or digital); arranging and sound design prefer a keyboard. 2. What are your space and neighbours like? Flat plus late hours — a digital with headphones is almost the only answer. 3. Will you move within five years? Moving an acoustic piano is expensive; a digital rides along.

One pragmatic suggestion

Limited budget, undecided direction: start with a digital piano. Its action carries you through the vast majority of intermediate material, with silence and portability included. Once you are sure "I will wrestle classical piano for life" or "I have a stable room", consider an acoustic — you will buy more accurately then.

One truth about "action"

Action is the core spec of any keyboard instrument, and it can only be measured by hand, not read from a spec sheet. Go to a shop and play three: a weighted digital, an acoustic, an unweighted keyboard — five minutes gives you your answer.

Listen: two neighbouring keys, music's smallest distance

A keyboard's layout is itself a theory diagram: neighbouring keys sit one semitone apart. Hear what those two neighbours sound like together — this is how the keyboard helps everyone understand music.
