I Know Nothing — Where Do I Start?

Three routes — pick the one matching what you care about most, not all three

"I want to learn music but do not know where to start" — this page gives you three routes. Pick one; do not read all three.

What you are trying to do here

Answer one question first: what is actually blocking you? The three routes match three different blockers.

Where to read: three routes

Route A: decide whether and what to learn

Take this if you have not even chosen an instrument.

  • Starting from zero: the first step — set a direction first
  • Is it too late as an adult? — real advantages and real obstacles
  • "Do I have talent?": a misframed question — why this question has no answer
  • Theory first, or instrument first? — they are not a queue

This route lives entirely in the midicn home site: thirty short pieces, one question each.

Route B: you have chosen an instrument but not how to practise

Take this if you have picked an instrument but do not know how to practise.

  • How long should I practise each day? — frequency beats duration
  • Theory first, or instrument first? — the two can run in parallel
  • Tool: metre: why 3/4 and 4/4 sound different — clickable
  • Offsite: the instrument guide — understand how your instrument makes sound first

Route C: you want to understand what music is

Take this if you would rather understand than start playing.

  • The music industry map: where the money is — see how music is organised
  • Why the major scale sounds happy: explanation and dispute — start from the simplest phenomenon
  • Offsite: music theory — over 150 theory entries

What is not built yet

Honestly, two gaps on this path:

1. The glossary is short of its plan — it should be the place where any term is one click away, and it is still a skeleton 2. There is no complete "zero to playing a piece" roadmap — the thirty guides cover judgement and mindset, but they do not cover the technical training track

The second point is a genuine gap. For now you have to assemble the guides and the instrument site yourself.
