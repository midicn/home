Streaming Economics — The Ledger of One Play

The numbers move; the structure does not — learn the structure

2 min readUpdated 2026-09-28

industrystreamingroyalties

Related

Musical FormPiano

"What is one play worth?" gets a new answer every year — and someone is always unhappy with it. The more useful question: what is the structure of that money, and down which pipes does it flow?

The inflow: subscriptions and ads

A streaming platform's revenue pool fills from two taps: subscription fees and advertising. The platform keeps operating costs and margin first; the rest enters the pool distributed to rights holders.

This structure explains a frequently misunderstood point: your streaming income depends on the pool's distribution rules, not just on your own play count.

Two rival rules

  • Pro rata: the whole pool is split by "your share of total plays". Most major platforms work this way.
  • User-centric: each subscriber's fee is split among only the artists that subscriber actually played.

The difference is large: under pro-rata, a listener who plays only independent music is also subsidising the platform's most-played content; user-centric tries to fix that. This is a live institutional argument — about how the pool is cut, not who decides.

The pipes

One play's money usually crosses several floors: platform → distributor/publisher → rights holders (label, publisher) → creators and performers under contract. Every floor takes its percentage and settles on its own schedule.

Two familiar experiences follow: decent play counts that look like tiny income (thin margins plus multiple floors), and money arriving much later than the plays (monthly settlement cycles).

The other side of "a hundred million plays"

Streaming is a thin-margin, high-volume world: each play is minuscule; only enormous quantity produces meaningful income. A new mindset followed — not "how many copies sold" but "how many plays accumulated".

It also shapes how music is written for it: fast openings, early choruses, smooth loops — not aesthetic decline, but a conversation with the way "one play" is counted.

Listen: the loop, the most familiar state

The definitive streaming-era behaviour is not "one listen" but "on repeat". Hear this beat loop four times — this is how play counts accumulate in daily life.
