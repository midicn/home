Music and Cultural Identity — Why "Our Songs" Matter

Belonging is not abstract; it hangs on particular songs

1 min read26 characters条目Updated 2026-09-29

ethnomusicologyidentitysociety

Related

MelodyGuqin

"This is our song." The "we" in that sentence is never just a word. It is a boundary, a belonging, something that — sung in the right occasion — makes certain people's eyes sting.

Why songs can carry identity

Because they provide belonging and distinction at once:

  • Singing the same line together shares something older and more direct than language (no lyrics needed to sing together);
  • And "this is ours, that is not" draws the line between groups.

The inside and the outside of an identity are built by the same songs.

Three common settings

1. Life thresholds — weddings, funerals, graduations: songs used solemnly at the unrepeatable moment (examples 1 and 2 above);

2. Shared history — suppressed memories, years of migration, a particular experience: the song as evidence of "we lived through it";

3. Daily company — not ritual, just "that's what our generation listened to"; nostalgia as identity (see music and memory).

An important complexity: identity flows

One melody can be claimed by several groups at once. Molihua is typical: sung differently in different places and contexts, each treating it as "ours".

This need not be a contest — it can be healthy coexistence: identity is not "one single rightful owner" but "this tune has a home in me".

The flip side is using song to draw an enemy line — when a song is used to prove who does not belong, identity stops being belonging and becomes exclusion. Reality rarely draws that line cleanly, which is why the discussion is complicated.

Attitudes worth practising

  • Do not speak for others: every group's "our songs" contain internal variation — no single song or version stands for everyone;
  • Respect the right not to join: in some traditions, "not singing" is a free choice (see fieldwork ethics);
  • Distrust the fantasy of "pure tradition": many "ancient songs" are centuries of borrowing and mixing (see the journeys of instruments).

Song's power is also its weight

A song can gather people and push them apart. That is not a flaw of music — it is the necessary two faces of any strong symbol. Mature use: to connect, not to fence.

Listen: a melody that can be sung together

Hear a mostly stepwise, easy-to-open line — no lyrics, yet dozens can sing it at once. That is the sound of identity forming.
