Melody

"I only quoted a little", "I made no money", "I credited the source" — the three most spoken lines in copyright arguments, and none of them is a free pass. The truth about fair use: it is a narrow door, and the mechanism at the door differs by country.

(An introduction to the rules; not legal advice.)

Two legislative roads

  • China: enumerated（closed list）. The law lists specific situations where use needs neither permission nor payment (personal study, appropriate quotation, news reporting, teaching and research, etc.), plus a catch-all. "It feels fair" beyond the list is usually not supported.
  • The United States: an open standard（four factors）. Whether something is fair use is judged by: ① purpose and character (transformative? commercial?) ② nature of the work ③ amount and substantiality used ④ effect on the original's market.

Both incorporate the international "three-step test": special cases only + no conflict with normal use + no unreasonable harm to the rightsholder.

Three popular misunderstandings

1. "Non-commercial = safe." Wrong. Commerciality is one factor (in the US) and does not enlarge China's closed list; 2. "I credited the source." Wrong. Attribution respects moral rights; it is not a licence; 3. "Only fifteen seconds." Short is not automatically safe. What you took (the most memorable hook) may matter more than how much.

A sense of scale for practice

Not legal standards — risk-management rulers:

  • Quotation to make a point (criticism, teaching, research) sits far safer than quotation as decoration or bait;
  • More transformative, safer (you give the material a new job); more substitutive, riskier (you do the original's job);
  • For short-video soundtracks, the most dangerous move is "unaltered + commercial account + full track" — three factors stacking, and fair use is almost surely gone.

The easy way out

Rather than studying exactly where the line runs, use material that needs no line-drawing: openly licensed music, public-domain works, royalty-free libraries (see the licensing family). The safest compliance is never walking the contested road.
