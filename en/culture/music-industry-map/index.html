The Music Industry Map — Where the Money Comes From

Two pools and one chain — draw the map before following the money

2 min readUpdated 2026-09-28

industrybusinessgeneral

Related

MelodyClassical Guitar

"The music industry" sounds like a single building. It is closer to a river system: two great pools, one main channel, and countless tributaries. Draw the map first; the money follows.

The two pools

Revenue flows broadly into two pools:

  • Recorded music: records, downloads, streams. The most industrialised part — money reaches platforms and labels first, then flows to creators through contracts.
  • Live and merchandise: tickets, festivals, merch, sponsorship. In recent years, the more dependable income for many musicians.

Plus a frequently forgotten pool: licensing (music used in film, ads, games) — not always large per deal, but the steadiest cash flow for "evergreen" repertoire.

The main channel: a song's journey

1. Writing: the songwriter creates a work (copyright is born with it);

2. Publishing: sheet music, licensing, royalty collection — the oldest and most overlooked link;

3. Recording: the work becomes a recording (a second, separate right);

4. Distribution: into shops, radio, platforms — today the barrier is nearly zero, and the competition is nearly infinite;

5. Consumption: someone listens — and a small coin drops somewhere along the chain.

Tributaries: what the middlemen do

  • Labels pay for recordings and promotion, taking a share of future income;
  • Distributors and platforms deliver music to ears, splitting by plays or subscriptions;
  • Collective management organisations collect public-performance money for creators (see performance rights and collective management);
  • Managers and promoters arrange concerts, fees and sponsorship.

Every middleman solves a real problem — and each also takes a slice. How to cut the cake is the industry's eternal argument.

A reminder: the map is not the territory

Vast amounts of music live outside the industry: folk songs, public-domain classics, community music, amateur work — not built for revenue, yet the groundwater the industry drinks from (see the second example below).
