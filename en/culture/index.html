Music in General — Seventy Guides

Seventy guides in six directions — one question each, and each says where to go next

1 min read0 characters页面Updated 2026-10-03

generalindexculture

"I want to understand this music thing" — these seventy guides run in six directions, and each one answers a single question.

They are not a required-reading list. Read the piece that matches whatever is actually blocking you, then follow the "Next" link at the end of it.

If you have no idea where to start, here is one allocation:

  • Want to know how music makes money and how it reaches you → begin with industry and business;
  • Want to know whether you may use a piece of music → go straight to copyright and licensing; that group is written entirely from real questions;
  • Want to know why music moves you → start with music and the mind;
  • Want to understand what music means in its own culture → ethnomusicology;
  • Want to learn an instrument, find a teacher, or make sense of what people say about practice → music education;
  • Want to know how sound travels from stage to ear, and from ear to a record → live and recording industries.

If still unsure, start with why the major scale sounds happy — it needs no prerequisite at all.

Industry and Business (12)

*How music turns into money — where it comes from, who gets it, what is changing.*

  • The Music Industry Map — Where the Money Comes From —— Two pools and one chain — draw the map before following the money
  • A Short History of Music Publishing — Business on Paper —— Before records, the music business was made of paper
  • A Short History of the Record Industry — Wax to Streaming —— Every new medium rearranges the entire industry
  • The Album Era — How It Began and How It Faded —— The album is a child of a medium — and the child has grown up
  • Streaming Economics — The Ledger of One Play —— The numbers move; the structure does not — learn the structure
  • The Live Business — Tickets, Venues and Tours —— Live is the industry's oldest income — and its fastest-growing in recent decades
  • How Independent Musicians Make a Living —— Not one income but a puzzle of a dozen pieces
  • The Licensing Family — Know the Table Before Using Someone's Music —— Three licences, two paths — how to use music legally, mapped once
  • From Radio to Algorithm — How Music Reaches You —— Every generation is fed by a gatekeeper — and the gatekeeper keeps changing shape
  • Charts and Awards — How Rankings Are Counted —— Charts are statistics; awards are judgement — two kinds of authority, two mechanisms
  • The Instrument-Making Industry — A Fiddle's Journey —— From forest to stage — instruments are products of wood, metal and human hands
  • The Chinese Music Market — A Path of Its Own —— From ringtones to short videos — a distinct route through the same industry

Copyright and Licensing (14)

*How many rights sit on one song, who may use it, and where the edges are.*

  • How Many Rights Live in One Song —— Work, recording, performance — one song is a stack of things in law
  • Moral Rights and Economic Rights — What Kind of Right Is a Name —— Copyright holds two temperaments — one about money, one about the name
  • Copyright Duration — 50 Years, 70 Years and the Public Domain —— Protection is finite — and finiteness is by design
  • Public-Domain Reckoning — When a Piece Is Free to Use —— Public domain is not "old" but "expired" — four gates to check
  • Fair Use and Fair Quotation — Where the Line Runs —— "Not sued" is not "legal" — fair use is a narrow door
  • Neighbouring Rights — Performers and Producers —— Copyright protects creation; neighbouring rights protect those who turn it into sound
  • Covering vs Copying — Two Different Tracks —— Re-singing and copying a recording are legally different acts
  • Performance Rights and Collective Management — Who Collects the Shop's Music Money —— Deals too many to make one by one — the job of collective societies
  • Music for Video — How Sync Licensing Actually Works —— "Sync" is trade talk — setting music onto picture, synchronised
  • Sampling and Transformation — Moving Sound Itself —— The most radical quotation — it moves the sound itself
  • The Creative Commons Family — How to Read the Six Combinations —— Standardised licences — freedoms granted up front
  • Open Music Data and Licensing — Doing Datasets Right —— A dataset is a collection of works — its licence answers four questions
  • Chinese Copyright Law for Music — Context and Key Points —— To read music copyright in China, hold onto a few local anchors
  • Copyright Myths — Ten Claims, Ten Replies —— Each sits between "sounds right" and "is right" — taken one by one

Music and the Mind (12)

How music gets into feeling — emotion, memory, focus, body.

  • Why Does Major Sound "Happy"? Explanations and Debates —— One popular answer, one more careful answer, and one honest one
  • Perfect Pitch — Gift, Training, and Its Edges —— The ability to name a note is not the ability to know music
  • Music and Memory — Why Old Songs Carry Old Summers —— Music is a time machine — hearing, feeling and scene packed as one
  • Earworms — How a Melody Gets Stuck —— Not a faulty brain — a memory system running normally, minus the off switch
  • How Music Moves Us — Expectation and Resolution —— Music carries no feelings — it builds expectations, then keeps, delays or breaks them
  • Music and Language — Two Roads in the Brain —— Shared circuits, separable systems — more alike, and more distinct, than expected
  • Rhythm and the Body — Why We Cannot Keep Still —— The first organ for hearing rhythm is not the ear — it is the body
  • Music Therapy — What It Is, and What It Isn't —— A trained clinical profession — not "music as medicine", not a plural of "music is good for you"
  • How Musical Taste Gets Built —— Taste is not hierarchy but biography — and its sources are traceable
  • Background Music — Working With Music On —— Neither a focus hack nor an efficiency poison — it depends on task, person, music
  • Synesthesia — Hearing Colours —— A minority truly see sound — and most of us carry a watered-down version
  • What Practice Does to the Brain —— Practice is a rewiring project — and a chance to clear up some famous myths

Ethnomusicology (12)

*How music lives inside people’s lives — fieldwork, transmission, variation, ritual.*

  • What Is Ethnomusicology — From Comparing to Understanding —— It studies music — and how music lives inside whose life
  • Fieldwork Ethics — Whose Voice, Whose Rights —— Five things to settle before you press record
  • Oral and Written — Two Worlds of Transmission —— A score preserves notes, not how to make them live
  • Music and Cultural Identity — Why "Our Songs" Matter —— Belonging is not abstract; it hangs on particular songs
  • The Problem with the Label "World Music" —— A label everyone buys often holds two different things
  • A Short History of Field Recording — Wax to Hard Drive —— Who records, what, and for whom — every era re-asks
  • The Journey of an Instrument — It Becomes Another One Abroad —— What travels with an instrument is not just its shape but the problem it solved
  • Scales Around the World — Not Just Twelve-Tone Equal —— The piano is a choice, not a law
  • Work and Song — the Movement Is the Meter —— Hauling, felling, transplanting each have their own beat; singing carries it
  • Folk Variants — There Is No Original —— The same tune grows differently in different hands; that is not decay
  • Ceremony and Music — Why a Melody Cannot Be Changed Casually —— In ceremony music is not expression; it is part of the procedure
  • How Recording Changed Folk Music —— A recording fixes one of many versions; that is both a gain and a loss

Music Education (10)

*How to learn and how to teach — methods, practice, exams, teachers and students.*

  • Three Teaching Methods — Dalcroze, Orff, Kodály —— They differ not in difficulty but in what they believe music is
  • The Suzuki Method — Learning Music Like a First Language —— Listen first, imitate second, read the score last — a route that has been tested
  • Teaching Ear Training — From Hearing It to Writing It —— The middle step — hearing it in your head — is the real threshold
  • Exam Systems — What Each One Actually Tests —— An exam is not a difficulty scale but a sample of abilities; read which abilities
  • Group Lessons and One-to-One — Who Each Suits —— Group lessons provide rehearsal; one-to-one provides diagnosis
  • Judging a Good Teacher — and a Good Student —— In a trial lesson, listen not to how well they play but to whether they can diagnose you
  • Adult Music Education — A Completely Different Design —— Adults are not learning skills but the ability to finish something interrupted
  • What School Music Lessons Actually Teach —— What a music lesson really contains is a listen-do-write cycle
  • The "Reading First or Playing First" Debate —— The debate is misplaced — the real disagreement is not order but what notation is
  • What Practice Research Actually Says —— The "ten thousand hours" line is widely quoted; the research does not say that

Live and Recording Industries (10)

*How sound travels from stage to ear, to microphone, to finished product.*

  • Hall Acoustics — How a Good Sound Is Designed —— A concert hall is not a soundproof room; it is an instrument built for specific music
  • Live Sound — How Sound Gets to Your Ears —— Reinforcement is not amplification; it fixes what instruments cannot do
  • A History of Recording — From Acoustic to Digital —— Each technical step solved an old problem and created two new ones
  • Roles in the Studio — Producers and Engineers —— The division separates aesthetic judgement from technical execution, but each must persuade the other
  • Microphone Basics — Dynamic, Condenser, Direction —— A microphone really decides what it listens to, not what it costs
  • Mixing and Mastering — The Last Two Steps —— Mixing is subtraction inside a balance; mastering is compromise inside constraints
  • Live and Studio — Two Worlds of Sound —— The live version's value is that it cannot be repeated — which is exactly why it is technically worse
  • Stage and Monitors — How a Show Gets Wired —— What musicians hear and what the audience hears are two different things
  • The Vinyl Revival — Why an Old Medium Came Back —— It returned not for fidelity but for weight, sequence, and because it will not skip for you
  • Sound in the Streaming Era — What Loudness Normalisation Does —— Part of the loudness difference you hear is a platform rule, not a musician's choice

About these guides

  • The "Next" link at the end of each one is deliberate: it sends you where you actually need to go, not somewhere arbitrary;
  • each one has listenable examples drawn from the dataset, labelled with source and licence tier;
  • each one is bilingual, with the two versions agreeing on every claim and fact.

If something here reads wrong to you, or you want to offer a counter-example, we welcome it — general-audience writing is most at risk when nobody pushes back.
