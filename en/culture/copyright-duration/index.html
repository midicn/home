Copyright Duration — 50 Years, 70 Years and the Public Domain

Protection is finite — and finiteness is by design

Related

Melody

"How long until anyone may use it?" — the answer is a table whose core logic fits in one sentence: protection is finite.

(An introduction to prevailing rules; not legal advice.)

Why a limit must exist

If rights were eternal, common culture would be locked: nobody could reprint Shakespeare, adapt folk songs, or re-record the classics. So the system draws a line between incentive to create and feeding the public domain — cross it, and the work enters the public domain, free for all.

The two common gears: 50 and 70 years

SystemGeneral rule
ChinaLife of the author + 50 years after death (to 31 December of that 50th year)
US / EULife + 70 years (extended in the US by the 1998 act)

The same work may be free in China and protected in the US — which is why cross-border use follows the rules of the place of use.

The arithmetic for special works

  • Joint works: counted from the last surviving author;
  • Works of legal entities (owned by a company, etc.): usually 50 years from first publication (Chinese rule);
  • Anonymous works: 50 years from publication (Chinese rule);
  • Performances and sound recordings carry their own clocks (recordings: 50 years from fixation in China, with refinements after the 2020 amendment — check the current text for real cases).

The three most common traps

1. A public-domain work ≠ the recording is public: a 19th-century tune is free, but a 1950s recording of it may still be protected — score and sound are two different things (see how many rights live in one song); 2. Public domain ≠ no duties at all: attribution and integrity remain protected without a term limit under Chinese law — the author's name is still the author's name; 3. Adaptations grow an onion: adapt a free work and your adaptation creates new rights of your own — layers wrap layers; users must watch the newest skin.
